# Operations that are not EVM transactions

## Question

> v0.1 handles EVM transfers and allowlisted contract calls. Hyperliquid and Polymarket orders are signed off-chain messages (EIP-712 typed data); swaps are routed contract calls; other chains (Solana) have other transaction shapes. What must an intent, a policy and the signer's decoder look like for each, what does `@open-wallet-standard/core` 1.4.5 support, and which venue is the smallest honest first step?

## Answer

- **One rule covers every venue.** An operation is an intent with closed, typed fields. The person approves the intent's hash. The signer builds the venue's signed object (transaction, L1 action, order struct) from those fields with its own code for that one venue, chooses the few values that may vary (nonce, expiry), and signs. It never accepts typed data, a message or bytes from the caller. "Decoder" is the wrong word for the safe design: the signer is an encoder, and the only decoding is the tests that compare its output with the venue's reference implementation.
- **The signer gains no generic typed-data route.** Each venue adds one builder module in `apps/signer/src/venues/` and one typed method on the `Vault` interface. The v0.1 gate "no route for messages, typed data or hashes" stays true as stated, because no route accepts them; the signer produces them only from an approved intent.
- **Caps change unit, not shape.** For an order the measurable spend is its worst-case notional, `size × limit price`, in the venue's quote unit, counted when signed. Leverage and margin live in venue state the signer cannot read; the real bound on loss is what the wallet deposited into the venue, and that deposit is a v0.1 EVM transfer under v0.1 caps.
- **"Exactly one" holds at the signer for every venue.** Whether it also holds at the venue, and for how long a signed order stays usable, differs. Hyperliquid enforces a per-signer nonce set and an optional `expiresAfter` that the signer can set itself. Polymarket CLOB V2 orders carry no signed expiry and no per-order on-chain cancel. A Solana transaction expires with its recent blockhash.
- **OWS 1.4.5** signs EIP-712 typed data for any `eip155:<n>` chain argument, transactions for every chain family it supports (13 by its own count, Solana among them), raw hashes, EIP-7702 authorizations and NEAR NEP-413 messages. For EVM transactions, typed data and Solana it gives its policy engine nothing decoded beyond the chain argument, a few typed-data domain fields and the raw typed-data JSON for executable policies (F2.2, F2.6, F2.8). It is a usable key store for all of the above and an authority for none.
- **Smallest honest first step:** Hyperliquid limit orders on testnet, signed by the wallet's own key, with a signer-chosen nonce, a signer-chosen `expiresAfter`, a client order id derived from the approval id, and cancels by that id. A signer-built EVM swap is smaller still and should be its own ticket, but it is still an EVM transaction and does not answer this question. Polymarket gives the weakest guarantees of the four: since April 2026 its orders have no signed expiry, new accounts trade through a contract wallet, and API access needs a second typed-data signature.

## Findings

### F1. What v0.1 signs, and the rule that must survive

1. There are three operation kinds: `evm.transfer`, `evm.erc20.transfer` and `evm.call`. **[source]** `packages/protocol/src/intent.ts:20`, `:28`, `:40`, union at `:48`.
2. An intent carries `chainId: ChainId`, and `ChainId` accepts only `eip155:<n>`. **[source]** `packages/protocol/src/primitives.ts:76-79`. It also requires `maxFeeWei`, a gas ceiling. **[source]** `packages/protocol/src/intent.ts:90`.
3. The `approve-intent` envelope binds `intentHash`, `walletId`, `agentId`, `chainId` and `policyHash`; its `chainId` is the same EVM-only type. **[source]** `packages/protocol/src/envelopes.ts:34-41`.
4. `evaluateIntent` checks wallet, requester, chain, fee cap, then the contract allowlist or recipient allowlist, then per-asset caps against a 24-hour ledger. A contract call that moves no native coin passes the caps with amount 0, which is why calls are denied unless the target is allowlisted. **[source]** `packages/protocol/src/evaluate.ts:30-79`; `docs/approval-protocol.md:219-235`.
5. The signer builds `to`, `value`, `data` and the chain from the intent and takes only `nonce`, `gas` and the two fee fields from the caller. **[source]** `apps/signer/src/signer.ts:677-708`; `packages/protocol/src/signer/api.ts:146-157`.
6. Inside one SQLite transaction the signer consumes the envelope nonce, evaluates the policy against its own ledger, signs, checks that the signature recovers to the wallet over exactly the built transaction, and records the signature and the spend. **[source]** `apps/signer/src/signer.ts:755-826`, `:105-110`.
7. The `Vault` interface has exactly one signing method, `signEvmTransaction`, and its comment says there is "deliberately nothing here for messages, typed data, raw hashes or EIP-7702 authorizations". **[source]** `apps/signer/src/vault.ts:12-26`.
8. The release gate: "the signer has no route for messages, typed data or hashes; the MCP tool list is exactly the seven tools above". **[source]** `specs/agent-workspaces-v0.1.md:461`. The decision behind it: "EVM only; ... no message or typed-data signing anywhere; one approval signs once". **[source]** `specs/agent-workspaces-v0.1.md:127-128`.
9. The protocol's versioning rule: "Any change to the shape or meaning of something that is hashed ... is a new version, never an edit in place." **[source]** `docs/approval-protocol.md:419-427`. Adding operation kinds touches this rule (see O5).

### F2. What `@open-wallet-standard/core` 1.4.5 supports

1. **Exports.** `signTransaction`, `signMessage`, `signTypedData` ("EVM only"), `signHash`, `signAuthorization` (EIP-7702), `signNep413Message` (NEAR), `signAndSend`, plus wallet, policy and API-key management. **[source]** `ows/index.d.ts:63-78`, `:104-105`.
2. **Typed data is EVM-only and takes JSON.** `sign_typed_data` refuses non-EVM chains, then for an `ows_key_` token parses the JSON, compares `domain.chainId` with the chain argument if the domain has one, evaluates OWS policy with a `TypedDataContext` (verifying contract, chain id, primary type, domain name and version, raw JSON) and signs. **[source]** OWS `ows/crates/ows-lib/src/ops.rs:862-901`; `ows/crates/ows-lib/src/key_ops.rs:256-330`.
3. **Any EVM chain number is accepted.** `parse_chain("eip155:1337")` falls through to a namespace match and returns an EVM chain. **[source]** OWS `ows/crates/ows-core/src/chain.rs:253-313`. So the Hyperliquid L1 domain (chain id 1337, F3.2) can be signed by passing `eip155:1337`.
4. **OWS's EIP-712 encoder is its own code, with gaps.** It is hand-written (`ows-signer/src/eip712.rs`). A `uint` given as a decimal string must fit `u128`; larger values must be hex. Values are not checked against the declared bit width: a hex value longer than 32 bytes is cut to its last 32 bytes, and a `uint8` field accepts any integer. **[source]** OWS `ows/crates/ows-signer/src/eip712.rs:237-372`. A Polymarket `tokenId` is a uint256 above `u128` **[INFERENCE]**, so it must be passed as hex. Consequence: the signer computes the digest itself (with viem) and checks the returned signature against that digest; a divergence then fails closed.
5. **OWS's "hyperliquid" is HyperEVM, not order signing.** The alias `hyperliquid` maps to `eip155:999`. **[source]** OWS `docs/07-supported-chains.md:65`, `:107`; `ows/crates/ows-core/src/chain.rs:237-239`. Nothing in OWS knows about HyperCore actions or Polymarket orders (no other match for either name in the clone) **[source]** (repository-wide search of the clone).
6. **Solana.** Every OWS wallet derives a Solana account (Ed25519, `m/44'/501'/0'/0'`). **[source]** `ows/README.md`, "Supported Chains". `signTransaction` for Solana requires the full serialized transaction envelope (compact-u16 signature count, signature slots, message), signs the message bytes as they are, and returns only the 64-byte signature; `transaction` in the result is set only for Midnight, so the caller places the signature in slot 0. **[source]** OWS `ows/crates/ows-signer/src/chains/solana.rs:69-99`; `ows/index.d.ts:20-29`; `ows/crates/ows-lib/src/ops.rs:687-701`. The agent-mode path builds a `TransactionContext` with no decoded effects for Solana (the trait default). **[source]** OWS `ows/crates/ows-signer/src/traits.rs:118-129`; `ows/crates/ows-lib/src/key_ops.rs:82-127`.
7. **One OWS wallet holds keys for every family.** `createWallet` derives addresses "for all chains"; the comment on `importWalletPrivateKey` says 15 accounts per `create_wallet`. **[source]** `ows/index.d.ts:38-52`. v0.1 keeps only the `eip155` account. **[source]** `apps/signer/src/vault.ts:116`. So each v0.1 wallet already has a Solana address under the same mnemonic, with no signer route that can spend from it **[INFERENCE]**.
8. **OWS policy remains no authority.** Earlier research found that EVM policy sees only the caller's chain argument, policy files are writable without a credential, and a token signs hashes, messages and typed data on its allowed chain. **[source]** `specs/research/ows-node-sdk-in-an-isolated-signer.md:29-32`. Nothing read for this report changes that.

### F3. Hyperliquid

1. **Two signing schemes.** Trading actions ("L1 actions": `order`, `cancel`, `cancelByCloid`, `updateLeverage`, ...) are hashed as `keccak256(msgpack(action) ‖ nonce as 8 bytes big-endian ‖ 0x00, or 0x01 ‖ vault address ‖ [0x00 ‖ expiresAfter as 8 bytes big-endian])`. **[source]** SDK `hyperliquid/utils/signing.py:174-185`. That hash becomes `connectionId` in a "phantom agent" `Agent(string source, bytes32 connectionId)`, `source` `"a"` on mainnet and `"b"` on testnet, signed as EIP-712 with domain `{name: "Exchange", version: "1", chainId: 1337, verifyingContract: 0x0}`. **[source]** `signing.py:188-213`, `:240-244`. Account actions (`usdSend`, `withdraw3`, `approveAgent`, `approveBuilderFee`, ...) are "user-signed": EIP-712 over the action's own fields, domain `HyperliquidSignTransaction` with a `signatureChainId` the signer picks, and a `hyperliquidChain` field `"Mainnet"`/`"Testnet"`. **[source]** `signing.py:216-237`, `:246-252`, `:81-153`, `:412-439`.
2. **Byte-exactness is the hard part.** Hyperliquid's own guidance: "It is recommended to use an existing SDK instead of manually generating signatures"; common errors are field order for msgpack, trailing zeros on numbers, and upper-case addresses; a wrong signature recovers some other address and the API says only "User or API Wallet ... does not exist". **[source]** [Signing](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/signing.md). Prices and sizes are strings formatted by `float_to_wire` (8 decimals, then trailing zeros removed). **[source]** `signing.py:475-483`. The SDK ships fixed vectors for the phantom agent and for order signatures with a known key, mainnet and testnet, with and without a cloid. **[source]** SDK `tests/signing_test.py:21-126` (MIT licence, `LICENSE`).
3. **Order fields.** `{"type":"order","orders":[{"a":asset,"b":isBuy,"p":price,"s":size,"r":reduceOnly,"t":{"limit":{"tif":"Alo"|"Ioc"|"Gtc"}} or {"trigger":{...}},"c":cloid?}],"grouping":"na"|"normalTpsl"|"positionTpsl","builder":{"b":address,"f":tenthsOfBps}?}`. `asset` is the perp index in `meta.universe`, or `10000 + index` for spot. `cloid` is an optional 128-bit hex. **[source]** [Exchange endpoint](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/exchange-endpoint.md), "Asset" and "Place an order"; SDK `signing.py:505-527`. There is no market order: the SDK's `market_open` sends an IOC limit at a slippage price. **[source]** SDK `hyperliquid/exchange.py:245-259`. Prices have at most 5 significant figures and `6 − szDecimals` decimals on perps; sizes are rounded to `szDecimals`. **[source]** [Tick and lot size](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/tick-and-lot-size.md). A minimum order value of $10 appears in the documented error response. **[source]** Exchange endpoint, "Place an order".
4. **Priority grouping spends HYPE.** A `grouping` of `{"p": n}` charges a priority fee from the account's staking balance. **[source]** [Priority fees](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/priority-fees.md). The signer must fix `grouping` to `"na"`.
5. **Nonces.** "The 100 highest nonces are stored per address. Every new transaction must have nonce larger than the smallest nonce in this set and also never have been used before." Nonces are tracked per signer and must lie within `(T − 2 days, T + 1 day)` of the block time. **[source]** [Nonces and API wallets](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/nonces-and-api-wallets.md).
6. **`expiresAfter`.** "Some actions support an optional field `expiresAfter` which is a timestamp in milliseconds after which the action will be rejected. User-signed actions ... do not support" it. **[source]** Exchange endpoint, "Expires After"; SDK `exchange.py:134-138`.
7. **API (agent) wallets.** A master account can approve API wallets to sign for it; an account has one unnamed and up to three named ones; a name can carry `valid_until` up to 180 days. Deregistered or expired agents may have their nonce state pruned, and "previously signed actions can be replayed once the nonce set is pruned"; Hyperliquid strongly suggests never reusing agent addresses. **[source]** Nonces and API wallets, "API wallet pruning"; Exchange endpoint, "Approve an API wallet".
8. **Builder codes.** An order may carry `{"b": builder, "f": fee in tenths of a basis point}`; the user must first sign `ApproveBuilderFee` with the main wallet, not an agent; fees are at most 0.1% on perps and 1% on spot; the builder needs 100 USDC of perps account value; a user can have 10 active builder approvals. **[source]** [Builder codes](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/builder-codes.md); Exchange endpoint, "Approve a builder fee".
9. **Margin and leverage.** Cross margin is the default; leverage is an integer from 1 to the asset's maximum, set by `updateLeverage {asset, isCross, leverage}`; "Leverage is only checked upon opening a position". **[source]** [Margining](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/margining.md); Exchange endpoint, "Update leverage".
10. **Cancels and status.** `cancel {a, o}` and `cancelByCloid {asset, cloid}` are L1 actions; an optional `f` must be omitted when false. `scheduleCancel` cancels all open orders at a time at least 5 seconds ahead, at most 10 triggers a day. **[source]** Exchange endpoint, "Cancel order(s)", "Cancel order(s) by cloid", "Schedule cancel". `orderStatus` takes an oid or a cloid and reports `open`, `filled`, `canceled`, `rejected` and about 25 specific cancel and reject reasons. **[source]** [Info endpoint](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/info-endpoint.md), "Query order status by oid or cloid".
11. **Funding and withdrawal.** The legacy bridge on Arbitrum (`0x2df1c51e09aecf9cacb7bc98cb1742757f163df7`) credits USDC sent to it to the sender's account; the minimum is 5 USDC and smaller deposits are lost. Withdrawal is a user-signed `withdraw3` with no Arbitrum transaction; a $1 fee. CCTP is now preferred and the legacy bridge is deprecated. **[source]** [USDC](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/usdc.md); Exchange endpoint, "Initiate a withdrawal request". New HyperCore accounts pay 1 quote token on the first transfer that names them as destination. **[source]** [Activation gas fee](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/activation-gas-fee.md).
12. **Testnet.** API at `https://api.hyperliquid-testnet.xyz`. **[source]** SDK `hyperliquid/utils/constants.py:2`. The faucet gives 1,000 mock USDC only to addresses that have deposited on mainnet. **[source]** [Testnet faucet](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/onboarding/testnet-faucet.md).

### F4. Polymarket (CLOB V2)

1. **V2 replaced V1 on 2026-04-28.** "Legacy V1 SDKs and V1-signed orders are no longer supported on production." Open V1 orders were wiped. **[source]** [Migrating to CLOB V2](https://docs.polymarket.com/v2-migration). Anything built from `py-clob-client` 0.34.6 (read above) is obsolete.
2. **The signed order.** `Order(uint256 salt, address maker, address signer, uint256 tokenId, uint256 makerAmount, uint256 takerAmount, uint8 side, uint8 signatureType, uint256 timestamp, bytes32 metadata, bytes32 builder)`, domain `{name: "Polymarket CTF Exchange", version: "2", chainId: 137, verifyingContract: 0xE111180000d2663C0091e4f400237545B87B996B}` (Neg Risk: `0xe2222d279d744050d28e00520010520000310F59`). `taker`, `expiration`, `nonce` and `feeRateBps` were removed; fees are set by the operator at match time; `timestamp` (ms) gives uniqueness and "is not an expiration"; `expiration` stays in the POST body for GTD but is not signed; builder attribution is the signed `builder` field. **[source]** v2-migration, "EIP-712 domain", "EIP-712 Order type", "POST /order body"; `ctf-exchange-v2/src/exchange/libraries/Structs.sol:25-57`.
3. **No per-order on-chain cancel.** The exchange records `orderStatus[orderHash]` and refuses a filled order. **[source]** `ctf-exchange-v2/src/exchange/mixins/Trading.sol:22`, `:46-50`. The only user-side on-chain stop is `pauseUser()`, which pauses every order of the caller from `block.number + userPauseBlockInterval` (default 100 blocks, at most 302,400). **[source]** `src/exchange/mixins/UserPausable.sol:11-38`; checked on match at `Trading.sol:62`. The migration guide calls this "operator-controlled `pauseUser` / `unpauseUser`", but the contract lets any user call both for themselves **[source]** (same file). Off-chain, cancellation is a request to the CLOB: "You can cancel orders at any time before they're matched via the CLOB API". **[source]** [Order lifecycle](https://docs.polymarket.com/concepts/order-lifecycle.md), "Cancellation".
4. **Signature types:** `EOA`, `POLY_PROXY`, `POLY_GNOSIS_SAFE`, `POLY_1271` (contract wallets). **[source]** `Structs.sol:59-68`. Accounts deployed on or after 2026-05-04 use a "Deposit Wallet", a smart wallet created through Polymarket's relayer with a Builder API key; an EOA trades directly only "if your EOA is allowlisted for trading". A Deposit Wallet owner can authorize a session signer with an expiry of up to 180 days and venue scopes. **[source]** [Wallets and authentication](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/wallets-auth.md), "Wallet Types", "Set Up EOA Trading"; [Session keys](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/session-keys.md).
5. **API keys need a second typed-data signature.** L1 auth signs `ClobAuth(address, string timestamp, uint256 nonce, string message)` in domain `ClobAuthDomain` version `"1"`, chain 137, with the fixed message "This message attests that I control the given wallet"; `derive-api-key` returns the same credentials for the same nonce; later requests use L2 HMAC headers with the API secret. V2 left this unchanged. **[source]** v2-migration, "EIP-712 domain" note; py-clob-client `signing/eip712.py:7-28`, `headers/headers.py:16-61`; wallets-auth, `clobAuthTypedData`.
6. **Collateral is pUSD**, wrapped from USDC.e through a Collateral Onramp; trading needs ERC-20 and CTF approvals to the exchange and, for Neg Risk markets, the Neg Risk Adapter. **[source]** v2-migration, "New collateral token"; [Contracts](https://docs.polymarket.com/resources/contracts.md).
7. **Geographic restrictions are published.** Close-only on frontend and API for, among others, BE, DE, FR, IT, PL, SK, GB and US; close-only on the frontend only for IE and NL. **[source]** [Geographic restrictions](https://docs.polymarket.com/api-reference/geoblock.md). This report does not interpret what that means for the project (see "Owner questions").

### F5. Swaps

1. v0.1 can already send a swap as an `evm.call` to an allowlisted router, but the caps see only the native value, and the threat model says an allowlisted router "that holds or can pull the wallet's tokens ... is outside the caps". **[source]** `docs/threat-model.md:54-59`; `packages/protocol/src/evaluate.ts:61-66`.
2. Uniswap's SwapRouter02 takes `exactInputSingle((tokenIn, tokenOut, fee, recipient, amountIn, amountOutMinimum, sqrtPriceLimitX96))`, and `amountIn = 0` makes the router swap its own balance. A deadline comes from `multicall(uint256 deadline, bytes[] data)`. **[source]** [IV3SwapRouter.sol](https://github.com/Uniswap/swap-router-contracts/blob/main/contracts/interfaces/IV3SwapRouter.sol); [IMulticallExtended.sol](https://github.com/Uniswap/swap-router-contracts/blob/main/contracts/interfaces/IMulticallExtended.sol) line 15.
3. A swap stays an EVM transaction: v0.1's nonce, receipt and at-most-once semantics hold unchanged **[INFERENCE]**. What changes is that the signer must encode the call itself from `tokenIn`, `tokenOut`, `fee`, `amountIn > 0`, `minAmountOut` and `deadline`, with `recipient` fixed to the wallet, so the cap can count `(tokenIn, amountIn)` and the person approves a guaranteed minimum out. Aggregator calldata (0x, 1inch, Universal Router command bytes) would have to be decoded, not built, and its formats change per release **[INFERENCE]**.
4. A router that is a swap target must not also be an `evm.call` target, for the same reason v0.1 forbids a capped token from being a call target (a raw call would skip the min-out and cap checks). **[source]** for the existing rule: `packages/protocol/src/policy.ts:117-131`; `specs/agent-workspaces-v0.1.md:150-151`.

### F6. Solana

1. The signer would build the message (header, account keys, recent blockhash, instructions) from intent fields, ask OWS to sign it inside a full envelope (F2.6), and splice in the signature. **[INFERENCE]** from F2.6.
2. What may vary after approval is the recent blockhash and the compute-unit price and limit, under a lamport fee ceiling the person saw; this mirrors v0.1's `TransactionParams` **[INFERENCE]**.
3. A transaction is valid only while its recent blockhash is recent (about 150 blocks), or indefinitely with a durable nonce account. **[INFERENCE]**: primary pages ([confirmation](https://solana.com/developers/cookbook/transactions/confirmation), [durable nonces](https://solana.com/docs/core/transactions/durable-nonces)) returned HTTP 429 or a challenge page when fetched for this report and were not re-read.
4. The control plane would need a Solana RPC client for balances, simulation, broadcast and confirmation, and the policy would need Solana assets (SPL mints) and base58 addresses: a second chain family through every layer **[INFERENCE]**.

Consequences for the design, each **[INFERENCE]** from the table and F1:

- The spend is recorded when the signer signs, as in v0.1 (`docs/threat-model.md`, "A signed amount counts against the daily cap even if the transaction never lands"). The signer never reads the venue, so a fill report from the control plane can never raise a limit.
- An order that only reduces a position should not count against the daily cap. If it did, an exhausted cap would stop the agent from proposing to close.
- For Hyperliquid the signer can make "not later than" true by itself: it sets `expiresAfter` a short window after signing. For Polymarket V2 it cannot: a signed order is a standing instrument until it fills or the account is paused.
- A deterministic client order id (from the approval id) lets the control plane find an order after a crash between submit and record, and the venue's nonce rule makes a resubmission harmless.
- Asset indices are what is signed; names are display. The coin name for each index must come from the passkey-approved policy, not from live venue metadata the control plane fetched.

### O2. How the signer obtains what it signs

1. **Accept typed data from the caller and check it** (decode JSON, compare fields with the intent). Rejected: the check must be complete for every field and every future field of every venue's type, and one miss signs something unapproved. It also reintroduces a typed-data route.
2. **Build from fields, per venue, inside the signer** (recommended). The signer is the only producer of the signed object. Correctness is a testing problem (vectors from the venue's reference SDK), and a wrong encoding fails closed: the venue recovers some other address and rejects (F3.2).
3. **Rely on OWS policy** (`allowed_typed_data_contracts`, executable policies). Rejected for the reasons in F2.8.

Within option 2, hand the typed data to OWS `signTypedData` (recommended) rather than computing the digest and calling `signHash`. The signer computes the digest with viem as well and checks the recovered address against it, as v0.1 does for transactions (F1.6). `signHash` would put a raw-hash method on the `Vault` interface, which v0.1 deliberately does not have (F1.7).

### O3. Which key signs Hyperliquid actions

1. **The wallet's own key** (recommended). The Hyperliquid account is the wallet's EVM address, deposits from it are credited to it (F3.11), and there is no extra approval action.
2. **A separate API wallet** approved by `approveAgent`. It cannot withdraw, but inside one signer process it isolates nothing, it adds an approval action, and deregistration can prune its nonce state so that old signed actions replay (F3.7).

### O4. Who may cancel a signed order

1. **The order approval includes the right to cancel that order** (recommended). The approval card says so. The signer signs `cancelByCloid` for an order it signed under that approval, on the control plane's request, records it in the decision log, and signs nothing else. A cancel moves no funds; for limit orders (no stop orders in the first step) it only removes an order the person approved.
2. **Every cancel needs a passkey.** Too slow for orders and adds an approval per cancel.
3. **Only `scheduleCancel`.** Cancels everything at once and is limited to 10 triggers a day (F3.10).

Stop and take-profit orders change this: cancelling a stop-loss removes protection the person relied on. They are out of the first step; when added, their cancel needs its own approval.

### O5. How the protocol absorbs new kinds

1. **Additive within version 1** (recommended). New operation types, a widened `ChainId`-like network type for intents and the `approve-intent` envelope, and an optional `venues` member in the policy leave every existing digest and test vector unchanged, because RFC 8785 omits absent members and existing values keep their bytes **[INFERENCE]**. A v0.1 signer refuses the new shapes, because every schema is strict. `docs/approval-protocol.md` §12 is amended to say this.
2. **Protocol version 2.** Cleaner on paper, but every hashed structure, tag and test vector changes, and the signer must carry two versions during migration.

## Recommendation

This is a recommendation, not a decision. The owner's open questions are listed below it.

1. Add one generic shape to the protocol: an intent's operation may target a venue network (`hyperliquid:testnet`, `hyperliquid:mainnet`) instead of an EVM chain; venue operations carry no `maxFeeWei`; the policy gets an optional `venues.hyperliquid` member. Additive within version 1 (O5.1).
2. First venue: Hyperliquid perpetual limit orders on testnet only (O1.A). The wallet's own key signs (O3.1). The signer builds the action from fields, fixes `grouping` to `"na"`, derives the cloid from the approval id, picks the nonce (monotonic per wallet and network) and `expiresAfter` (policy window, at most 300 seconds), and hands typed data with chain `eip155:1337` to OWS (O2.2). The order approval includes its cancel (O4.1). A withdrawal back to the wallet's own address, approved by passkey, completes the loop.
3. Caps: per-order and rolling 24-hour notional in micro-USD, counted at signing; reduce-only orders count against the per-order cap only. Asset indices and coin names come from the passkey-approved policy. No builder fee in the first step.
4. Do the signer-built swap (O1.C) as its own ticket. It closes the router gap that `docs/threat-model.md:54-59` already names, and it is the smallest change of all four.
5. Do not start Polymarket until the owner answers the questions below and there is a way to bound a signed order's lifetime. Do not start Solana until there is a reason beyond coverage.

The Draft ExecPlan for option A is [`specs/non-evm-operations.md`](../non-evm-operations.md).

## Owner questions (legal and geographic; EU first)

These are questions, not answers. This report does not say what the law is anywhere.

1. May an operator in an EU member state run software that places perpetual-futures orders on Hyperliquid for members of a workspace? Relevant frames to put to counsel: MiCA, the MiFID II perimeter for derivatives, ESMA's product-intervention history for CFD-like products to retail clients, and national law of the operator's and each member's state. Do [Hyperliquid's terms of use](https://app.hyperliquid.xyz/terms) permit API access from those jurisdictions?
2. The same question for prediction-market orders on Polymarket, including national gambling law, and given that Polymarket's published list makes Belgium, France, Germany, Italy, Poland and Slovakia close-only on its API and Ireland and the Netherlands close-only on its frontend (F4.7). Polymarket's co-location terms distinguish "third-party applications executing trades on behalf of end users"; does a self-hosted workspace count?
3. Should the open-source project ship venue support enabled, disabled by default with the operator enabling it, or not at all until the first two answers exist? Should the software call a venue's own geoblock endpoint before enabling a venue for a workspace?
4. Outside the EU: the same questions for the UK and the US, both on Polymarket's close-only list.

## Limits

- Nothing was run. No signature in this report was produced or verified; the encoding claims rest on reading the Hyperliquid Python SDK and its own test vectors, not on reproducing them.
- The Hyperliquid and Polymarket documentation is mutable and was read on one day. Polymarket changed its order format, collateral and wallet model between April and May 2026 (F4.1, F4.4); the same could happen again.
- The Solana facts in F6 are from general knowledge; the primary pages could not be fetched (HTTP 429).
- No legal or regulatory question was answered or researched beyond reading the venues' own published restriction pages.
- The OWS typed-data encoder (F2.4) was read, not tested; whether it matches viem for the Hyperliquid `Agent` type is the first thing the plan's tests check.
- Whether perp asset indices on Hyperliquid stay stable across listings and delistings was not established from a primary source; the plan treats a policy's index-to-name binding as the authority and checks it against live metadata only for early warning.
